The paper compares MobileNetV2 (ImageNet-pretrained, fine-tuned) against MobileViT-XXS and MobileViT-XS (trained from scratch) on two public Kaggle datasets — Butterflies-Austria-20 (20 classes, 6,479 images) and Scene Classification (6 classes, 17,034 images). All models are converted to TFLite with full-integer INT8 post-training quantization and evaluated for test accuracy before/after quantization (Table 4), then deployed on an ESP32-S3 (Table 5) and a Raspberry Pi 5 (Table 6). MobileNetV2 attains the best INT8 accuracy (0.9527 butterflies; 0.9129 scenes) and by far the lowest microcontroller latency (~27–36 s vs. ~400–493 s for MobileViT), while on the Raspberry Pi 5 all models run below 100 ms per image. The authors conclude MobileNetV2 offers the best overall trade-off, with MobileViT viable only on the more capable platform.

**Strong points.**
1. Relevant, practical topic for the Edge AI / TinyML community, with evaluation on *real* hardware rather than proxy FLOP counts — the ESP32-S3 memory-arena and latency measurements for MobileViT (Table 5, p. 9) are data rarely reported in the literature.
2. The full pipeline (training → INT8 PTQ → on-device functional validation) is coherent and clearly described at a high level, including the pre/post-quantization accuracy-drop analysis (Table 4, Sec. 5.2, p. 8).
3. Honest reporting: the authors explicitly state that the embedded evaluations used very few samples and should be read as functional validation only (Sec. 5, p. 8), and disclose the tflite_runtime/tensorflow runtime substitution on the Raspberry Pi 5 (p. 9).
4. Two complementary visual domains (fine-grained vs. coarse scene recognition) make the study design conceptually sensible, and dataset statistics are fully tabulated (Tables 1–3) and internally consistent.

**Weak points.**
1. **Confounded architecture comparison (critical).** MobileNetV2 benefits from ImageNet pretraining plus two-stage fine-tuning (Sec. 4.4, p. 7), while both MobileViT variants are trained from scratch on small datasets. ViT-hybrid models are known to be data-hungry; the reported accuracy ranking therefore reflects the training protocol at least as much as the architecture. Input resolutions (Sec. 4.1, p. 6) and augmentation policies (Sec. 4.2, p. 6) also differ per model/dataset. Either pretrain all models (ImageNet-pretrained MobileViT weights are publicly available) or train all from scratch, and hold resolution/augmentation constant — otherwise the central claim of Secs. 5.1 and 6 is not supported.
2. **Duplicated class in the butterfly dataset.** Table 2 (p. 4) and Figure 1 (p. 5) contain both "Iphiclides Podalirius" (297 images) and "phiclides Podalirius" (349 images) — the same species under two labels. This contradicts the statement that no problematic samples were identified (Sec. 4.1, p. 5), inflates the class count from 19 to 20, and makes the "fine-grained" accuracy figures hard to interpret (the classifier may be exploiting source artifacts to separate identical species). Merge or investigate these classes and report the impact.
3. **No statistical rigor.** All results come from a single run with seed 42 (Secs. 4.1, 4.4); no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests are given, though several reported accuracy gaps are small relative to test-set sizes (~648 and ~1,703 images). ESP32-S3 latencies are averaged over 1–4 invocations (Table 5).
4. **Data leakage risk unexamined.** The butterfly images were "collected through Google searches" (p. 5); with a random 80/10/10 split, near-duplicate images may cross the train/test boundary. No deduplication check is reported.
5. **Runtime asymmetry on Raspberry Pi 5.** Comparing MobileNetV2 under `tflite_runtime` with MobileViT under full TensorFlow (p. 9) invalidates a direct latency comparison in Table 6; at minimum run MobileNetV2 under both runtimes to quantify the gap, or use the XNNPack-enabled full TF interpreter for all models.
6. **Only global accuracy is reported.** Given the acknowledged class imbalance (Sec. 4.1, p. 5), macro-F1, per-class precision/recall, and confusion matrices are needed — especially for the fine-grained task.
7. **Thin related work and no external comparison.** Ten references total; missing directly relevant work (see suggestions). No comparison with published results on the widely used 6-class scene benchmark, and no stronger CNN baseline (e.g., MobileNetV3, EfficientNet-Lite).
8. **Missing experimental details for reproducibility.** No learning-rate values, batch sizes, training hardware, TFLite Micro version, ESP32-S3 clock/PSRAM configuration, or code release. The claim that 35.6 s/inference is "approaching practical interactive use" (Sec. 5.2, p. 8) should be removed or reworded.

**Suggestions.**
1. Equalize the training protocol (pretraining, resolution, augmentation) across all architectures, or explicitly reframe the paper as comparing *deployment pipelines* rather than architectures.
2. Fix the butterfly dataset (merge the duplicated Iphiclides podalirius classes), run a near-duplicate/leakage check, and report per-class metrics and confusion matrices.
3. Report mean ± standard deviation over ≥3–5 seeds and apply a significance test (e.g., McNemar's test on paired test-set predictions) to accuracy comparisons; measure device latency over ≥100 invocations with warm-up.
4. Add and discuss the missing related work: MobileNetV3 (Howard et al., ICCV 2019); EfficientNet-Lite; MobileViTv2 (Mehta & Rastegari, 2022) and MobileViTv3 (Wadekar & Chaurasia, 2022) — the successors explicitly designed to reduce MobileViT latency; EdgeNeXt (Maaz et al., 2022); MobileOne (Vasu et al., CVPR 2023); MCUNet (Lin et al., NeurIPS 2020) and MLPerf Tiny (Banbury et al., 2021) for MCU-scale inference; and a quantization survey (Gholami et al., 2021). Complete the venues of Sandler et al. (CVPR 2018), Mehta & Rastegari (ICLR 2022), and Jacob et al. (CVPR 2018).
6. Investigate *why* MobileViT is ~400 s on the ESP32-S3 (reference-kernel fallbacks for attention ops in TFLite Micro vs. ESP-NN acceleration for convolutions) — this analysis would substantially raise the paper's value.
7. Release code, trained models, and conversion scripts; report all hyperparameters and hardware/runtime versions.
8. Unify the manuscript language: translate "Tabela/Figura/Referências", "Borboletas/Cenas", and "Queda" to English (or write the whole paper in Portuguese), and adopt one numeric format throughout.
